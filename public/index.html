Before you commit

What we vet, and why it matters

A business can look profitable on a seller's spreadsheet and still fail on tax, licensing or cash. Our report tests the claims against documents you can act on.

Cash flow & profitability

6 to 12 months of bank statements, M-Pesa records and sales ledgers reconciled to reported revenue. We flag seasonality, owner withdrawals and unrecorded cash.

KRA tax compliance

KRA PIN status, Tax Compliance Certificate, VAT and PAYE filing history, plus any outstanding returns, penalties or agency notices.

Licences & permits

Single business permit, county health clearance, liquor licence, NEMA, KEBS, sector-specific registrations — valid, current and transferable.

Lease & premises

Rent, service charge, remaining term, renewal and assignment clauses, landlord consent and any dilapidations exposure.

Stock, assets & debts

Physical stock count, asset register and hire-purchase checks, plus supplier and trade creditors, loans and staff liabilities.

Staff & statutory

Employment contracts, headcount and payroll, NSSF, SHIF and housing levy standing, and any pending labour or court claims.

Vetting is a paid service and is separate from the purchase price. The fee is quoted per business, agreed in writing before work starts, and is not a commission on the sale.

How it works

Four steps from interest to informed offer

Tell us what you want

Send an enquiry with the business, your budget and your timeline. We confirm the listing is still available.

Scope & fee

We agree the vetting scope and fee in writing, then issue a confidentiality undertaking before any seller data is shared.

Documents & site visit

We request the records, verify them with KRA and licence issuers, visit the premises and interview the seller.

Report & decision

You receive a written report with findings, red flags and a recommended offer range, then we can negotiate on your behalf.

Buyer protection

Vetting before you buy a business

A seller tells you the business turns over KES 300,000 a month. We check whether the bank account agrees. Cash flow, KRA compliance, licences, leases, stock and staff — verified against documents and confirmed with the issuing authority.

Scope of the report

What we check

Every engagement is scoped in writing, but a standard vetting covers the following:

1. Cash flow & profitability

6 to 12 months of bank and M-Pesa statements reconciled to sales records, invoices and stock purchases. We test the margin, look for seasonality, and strip out one-off income and owner drawings so the figure you rely on is trading cash flow, not a peak month.

2. KRA compliance

PIN status, Tax Compliance Certificate, VAT and PAYE registration and filing history, outstanding returns, penalties or agency notices, and whether any tax liability transfers with the business.

3. Licences & permits

Single business permit, county health certificate, food handling, liquor licence, NEMA and KEBS, sector registrations and professional memberships — each checked for validity, expiry and transferability to you.

4. Lease & premises

Rent and service charge, remaining term, renewal and assignment rights, landlord consent, deposit and dilapidations. A business is only worth what its premises allow it to keep earning.

5. Stock, assets & liabilities

Physical stock count and valuation, asset register, hire-purchase and logbook checks, supplier credit and trade creditors, loans, staff liabilities and any court or CRB records.

6. Staff & statutory

Headcount, contracts, payroll, NSSF, SHIF and housing levy position, plus any pending labour claims or disputes that would become yours on completion.

The process

From enquiry to report

We work for the buyer. The seller is not our client in a vetting engagement, so the findings in your report are not filtered through the person selling.

Enquiry

You tell us the business, your budget and the questions that worry you most. We confirm availability.

Scope & fee

We send a written scope and fixed fee. On acceptance, we issue a confidentiality undertaking to the seller.

Verification

Documents reviewed, KRA status and licences confirmed with the issuers, premises visited, stock counted.

Report

Written report, red flags and an offer range, followed by a call. Negotiation support is available if you want it.

Questions

Vetting FAQs

Why should a buyer pay for vetting?

Because the seller's figures are prepared by the seller. A vetting fee is small compared with buying a business whose KRA arrears, expired licences or overstated cash flow surface after completion. The report lets you walk away, renegotiate, or proceed with confidence.

Is the fee a percentage of the purchase price?

No. You are quoted a fixed fee for the work, based on the size and complexity of the business. There is no commission and no success fee on the purchase price.

How long does vetting take?

Typically 5–10 working days from the day we receive complete documents. Delay usually comes from the seller's records, so we tell you at the start what we need and chase it directly.

Do you guarantee the business is a good buy?

No. We verify documents and report facts, risks and inconsistencies. The commercial decision, and the price you are willing to pay, remains yours. We do not provide investment advice or guarantee future performance.

Will the seller know I am checking them?

For a live listing, yes — vetting happens with the seller's cooperation because we need their records. For an approach you make independently, we can act discreetly and only request what is necessary.

Can you help after the report?

Yes. We can negotiate with the seller, prepare the information for your advocate, and coordinate the licence transfers and handover checklist. Any additional work is agreed separately.

Sell your business, quietly and properly

Most business sales in Kenya fall apart at the vetting stage because the records were never ready. List with us and we prepare the business for a buyer's inspection — cash flow, KRA, licences and leases — before it goes to market.

What happens after you submit

1. Review

We read the submission and call you to confirm the figures and what documents you hold. Nothing is published at this stage.

2. Document check

We look at the KRA position, permit, licences and the records behind the sales and profit numbers.

3. Approval

If it is ready, we approve it and agree the asking price and listing fee with you. If it is not ready, we tell you what to fix first.

4. Publication

Approved listings are published on the Businesses for sale page with your identity withheld. Buyer enquiries come to us and are screened before you meet anyone.

What to have ready

  • Business name, location and what you sell
  • Monthly sales and profit for the last 6 to 12 months
  • KRA PIN, tax compliance certificate and returns status
  • Business permit and sector licences
  • Lease or title, rent and remaining term
  • Staff numbers, stock value and equipment list
  • Any loans, creditors or pending disputes
  • Your asking price and the reason for selling

Questions sellers ask

Before you list

Why is my submission reviewed before it is published?

Because a listing that cannot survive vetting damages the seller as much as the buyer. We check that the figures, KRA position, licences and liabilities are at least documented before your business is shown to buyers — that is the difference between a listing that sells and one that stalls.

Will my staff and competitors find out?

No. We do not publish your business name, address or photos without written consent. Buyers are screened and sign a confidentiality undertaking before they see financials or the exact location.

What if my records are incomplete?

Tell us. Missing M-Pesa records, untidy books or unfiled returns are common. We will tell you what to reconstruct before going to market, because a buyer's vetting will find it anyway.

Do I have to accept a vetting?

Buyers who are risking their savings will want independent checks, and we recommend they do. A business that can be verified sells faster and at a better price than one that cannot.

Can I sell shares instead of assets?

Both are possible, and the choice affects tax, licences and liabilities. We will flag the difference and ask you to take advice from your advocate and tax advisor before committing.
